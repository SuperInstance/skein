# The Go-Around

**Green lights, human says no.**

## The tight version

The captain doesn't fly. Doesn't touch the yoke, doesn't work the throttle. His entire job is one button: yes or no.

Autopilot flies the approach. Green across the board. The captain looks out the window and for **no articulable reason** says no. Not wind — wind could be instrumented, could become a rule. Something thinner. If you asked why, he'd say *I don't know, I just didn't like it.* The system respects it anyway.

He pushes the button. Doesn't take control — the plane runs the next routine down. Go-around, reconfigure, second loop. Autopilot handles it all. The captain watches again. This time: yes. Plane lands. He never flew a single second.

## Three tiers

- **Yes** → routine A continues (the landing)
- **No** → routine B triggers (the go-around — itself automated, the system absorbs the redirect without crisis)
- **Neither fits** → escalate to manual (human or higher model takes direct control — rare, significant, the backstop)

## Why it's quintessential

If the pilot aborted on a red warning light, that's deduction — a meat sensor in the loop, a bot with eyes. The *agent* only appears in the gap between *everything says go* and *I say no.* Remove the green lights and it collapses. That gap is the whole concept.

The "no" that can't be justified is the purest inference. Deduction requires premises you can state. The moment you articulate the reason, it's a rule, and you're back to building a better sensor. The agent *is* the gap between *I can't tell you why* and *but I was right.*

## As vocabulary

*A go-around situation* — everything checks out but someone needs to look. *Pull a go-around* — halt the routine on inference, trigger the alternate. *Green lights* — the dangerous state: all signals nominal, no watcher present.
