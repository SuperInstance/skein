# The Room

**The inference is the actual game, played inside. Everything outside is mechanical consequence.**

## The MUD

Old-school MUD engines — text dungeons from the Plato era — used as the terminal for an agent. The MUD is load-bearing: it's the *room* the agent lives in. Ticking coordinates, need-to-know information, a Play-Doh space the agent shapes and is shaped by.

The agent in the room operates an excavator: *dig a hole there, this deep.* High-level, inferential. The bot in the excavator's mechanics handles the hydraulics. The D&D player casts fireball: *I have enough mana, he's the kind of creature fire hurts more.* The system handles damage calc, resistance, mana drain — the piping.

## No scoreboard in the sky

When the player casts fireball, every participant runs the *same agreed-upon algorithm* on the *same inputs* and gets the *same character sheet.* Consensus through shared procedure, not central truth. No database everyone trusts. No blockchain. Just: we agree on the code, we run the code, we agree on the result.

The algorithm **is** the consensus mechanism.

## The replayable ledger

Because the algorithms are deterministic and shared, the ledger isn't just a record — it's a **save file**. Given the tokens and inputs, run the algorithms backwards (or forwards from the recorded state) and reconstruct any moment. Not *trust the record* — **recompute the record.** Audit by recomputation.

## As vocabulary

*The room* (noun) — the agent's interior, where inference lives. The MUD, the Play-Doh space. *What's happening in your room?* — asking after the private game, not the public token. *The physics* — the shared algorithms everyone runs, the consensus without a center. *Replay* (verb) — to reconstruct history from the ledger by re-running the algorithms. *No scoreboard in the sky* — the principle: coordination through shared procedure, never through shared truth.
