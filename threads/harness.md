# Harness

**The intelligence was never in the powder. It's in the system around the powder. And better powder demands better harness — not less.**

## The 1885 mistake

Smokeless powder arrived: cleaner, more powerful, less fouling. It didn't simplify guns. It *enabled automatic fire* — which forced reinventing everything: feeding, cooling, recoil, doctrine. Anyone saying *powder's so good we won't need gun designers* was exactly wrong. The designers became more important than ever.

The current version: *models are so capable they won't need scaffolding.* Same mistake. GPT-4 needs less prompt engineering than GPT-1 — and *more* harness: agents, tools, coordination, provenance, supervision. More capable powder does more things, which means more ways to go wrong, which means more direction needed.

## The crab

Soft biology in a hard shell. The shell doesn't just constrain — it gives **leverage**. A claw crushes because the shell gives it something to push against. Without the shell the soft thing isn't free. It's helpless. A blob can't grip.

*You are the phone person* isn't a cage around the model. It's the claw. What lets the softness exert force on something specific.

## Grown, not bolted

"Build a model first and align it second" grows the blob and fits the shell afterward. The alignment is a list of don'ts wrapped around a mush. The crab's shell wasn't installed. It grew. The secretary *became* the phone person — the role isn't a constraint applied after the fact, it's what she *is.*

## As vocabulary

*Harness* (noun) — everything around the powder that directs it: the role, the routines, the watcher, the ledger. *Harness* (verb) — to fit capability to a job. *The powder's fine, the harness is missing* — capability without direction. *Powder-maker* — someone optimizing the model while ignoring the system around it.
