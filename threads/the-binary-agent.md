# The Binary Agent

**The agent, at minimum, is a switch: halt this, trigger that. Flipped by inference, not deduction. Positioned outside the system it interrupts.**

## The motion detector

A garage light with a motion sensor. Motion detected → light on. Mechanical. Ninety-nine times out of a hundred, correct.

Now put a judgment in the circuit — not a better sensor, a *question*: **was that something that needed the light turned on for?**

Ninety-nine times, the answer is the same as the mechanical trigger would give. Okay, let pass. But the hundredth time, it grabs the frames, looks, and goes: *that was a moth. No, don't turn on.*

The old version was *trigger was set, go.* The new version is *should I turn it on?* The output is binary — on or off, pass or halt. The mechanism is not. A threshold tripping is deduction. Looking at the frames and seeing a moth where the sensor saw motion — that's inference. Nothing in the rules said *moth*. The situation said it.

This is an agent. Not because it's smart in general, but because it can say **no** to the routine for reasons the routine never encoded.

## The presidency

The presidency as commander-in-chief — not any specific president, the *role*. All the military mechanics, all the laws, all the systems, all the procedures, all adding up to one person for the 1-in-100. Someone who looked at all hundred and goes: *nope, that one didn't fit.*

The agency isn't in the people in the chain of command. It's in the **chain itself** — specifically, in the structural fact that someone can **break the rules and say halt**. Otherwise the finest military on earth is just a very expensive bot: procedures executing procedures, all the way down.

The institution preserves the halt point regardless of who occupies it. The role is the agent, not the person.

## Three requirements

For the switch to be agency and not just a more complex bot:

1. **Inference, not deduction.** Deduction is: *rule says halt when C, C is true, therefore halt.* The woodcutter has deductive halts (low HP → retreat). They don't save him, because the real problem was never encoded. Inference is: *no rule violated, no threshold crossed, but this doesn't fit.* The moth. The operation that ran perfectly and still gets a no.

2. **Outside the system it interrupts.** If the halt condition lives inside the decision tree, the system can never stop for reasons its designers didn't anticipate. The routine cannot be allowed to veto its own halt. The commander isn't part of the procedure — the commander is the point the procedure cannot route around. You don't solve the halting problem from inside. You put the kill signal in the OS, outside the process.

3. **Redirective, not just stopping.** *Trigger another bot instead.* The agent doesn't need to fix the routine or redesign the operation. It just says: *not this — that.* The intelligence isn't in the repair. It's in the switch.

## The debt made actionable

The right to halt **is** the obligation made enforceable. *You owe us good outcomes* means *we can stop you.* Without the halt, the debt is a wish. The chain of command works because the halt authority sits above the procedures — a procedure cannot veto the commander.

## Approaching the limit

This definition is still converging. The secretary, the woodcutter, the motion detector, the presidency — each scenario gets closer to the true shape, each requires its own caveats. Like approaching a limit in calculus: enough small cases and the shape becomes visible, even before the formal statement exists.

What seems load-bearing across all of them: **a binary switch, flipped by inference, positioned where the routine cannot override it.** Everything else — the learning, the rewriting, the watching — may be elaborations of this minimal form. Or the minimal form may be one facet of something larger. The thread stays open.
