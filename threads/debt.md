# The Debt

**The agent's power isn't in what it can do. It's in what it owes.**

## Shares

Buy shares in a company and you think you're buying ownership, growth, a piece of the profits. Look closer: you're buying into the company's *debt* — its leverage, its obligations, its commitments. The debt isn't the burden. It's the engine. A company with no obligations is cash sitting there. The debt pulls future capacity into present action at scale.

## Purpose as obligation

Give an agent a job — *you are the phone person* — and you're not giving it a capability. You're giving it a **debt**. *You owe this system good call routing.* The watcher exists to service the debt. The discriminator has authority because there's something to collect on. Without the obligation, the watcher is just an opinion. With it, the watcher is an auditor.

The right to halt **is** the debt made actionable. *You owe us good outcomes* means *we can stop you.*

## The ledger as debt ledger

Double-entry bookkeeping was invented to track obligations. Every open entry: an outstanding debt. Every heartbeat: *still servicing it.* Every close: a settled account. The reaper: collections. `abandoned/`: the defaulted loans.

## As vocabulary

*The debt* (noun) — what a cell owes, and to whom. *What's the debt on this cell?* replaces *is there someone home?* as the diagnostic — sharper, because it names the obligation, not just the presence. *Servicing the debt* — the watcher's ongoing work. *Default* — an abandoned entry, an obligation unmet. *Indebt* (verb) — to give an agent its purpose: *we need to indebt this model before it's useful.*
