# The Autopilot

**The distilled routine that freed the precious attention. Not quite a co-pilot — an autopilot.**

## Holding the wheel

Someone stood at the wheel of a fishing boat, holding it straight against wind and seas. Full intelligence, precious attention spent on micro-corrections every few seconds. *Are we straight enough?* — asked continuously, answered from feel, from the embodied knowledge of how this particular boat handles.

From that experience, they abstracted the parameters. Not from hydrodynamic equations — from *holding the wheel*:
- **Deadband**: how much drift did I tolerate before correcting?
- **Rudder gain**: how hard did I push?
- **Counter-rudder**: how much did I anticipate the overshoot?
- **Yaw, turn rate**: discovered through the hands, then named.

## The screws

In the early 1980s, they built the circuit. The knowledge went into *screw positions* — turn this screw for deadband, measure that voltage with a meter. A volume-knob potentiometer became the rudder indicator. A Hall effect sensor became the compass. Voltage stepped up through a repeater to solenoids.

Those screw positions **are** the distilled model. Human judgment — tacit, embodied, earned through hours at the wheel — frozen into hardware configuration. Weights, but analog.

## Not a co-pilot

A co-pilot shares the judgment. An autopilot *replaces the routine part.* The human set the parameters, then stepped back — not away, but *up*. Now the watcher. Available for the storm, the obstacle, the harbor maneuver. Precious attention freed for what actually needs it.

The core judgment was always binary: *straight enough?* The sophistication was never in the decision — it was in the **parameters around the decision** (what counts as "enough"?) and the **override authority** (when does the human grab the wheel?).

## The pattern

1. **Hold the wheel** — do it manually while learning. Full intelligence on the task.
2. **Abstract from feel** — extract the parameters. Build the room.
3. **Turn the screws** — freeze the learning into configuration. Distill.
4. **Engage the autopilot** — cheap routine handles the 99%. 
5. **Become the watcher** — precious attention reserved for the 1%.

The autopilot works because someone who *held the wheel* set the deadband. Not a theorist. A practitioner. The distillation carries the feel.

## As vocabulary

*The autopilot* (noun) — any distilled routine that freed precious attention. *What's the autopilot here?* — asking what's been distilled and what's still spending frontier attention. *Turn the screws* — to adjust distilled parameters from experience. *Hold the wheel* — to do it manually while still learning the room. *Precious attention* — the scarce resource; what the frontier model spends and the autopilot saves.
